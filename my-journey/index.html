01 THE BEGINNING · HARARE, ZIMBABWE

A place on the team.
A world beyond it.

At St George’s College, the first ambition was simple: make the athletics team. Triple jump became the way in. Then the possibility grew.

Before there was Du Airlines+

Chenge started triple jump in Form 3. In the school library, he studied elite jumpers on video. At home, he worked through the hop and step, using a mattress to practise. Before he had a place on the world stage, he was learning how to make a place on his school team.

02 THE EARLY YEARS · HOME PRACTICE

Watch. Try.
Find the rhythm.

A video could show him what was possible. Learning to do it took repetition, feedback and the belief to keep trying.

The work outside the spotlight+

Specialist coaching was not always available. Chenge remembers studying film, experimenting at home and learning to create structure for himself. That experience would later shape the academy: a young athlete should have a clear next step, even when the coach is not beside them.

Away from the runway, those school years also brought loss: his grandfather died during Lower Sixth.

Chenge’s account · TCU Magazine · Historical interview · Summer 2021

03 2017 · AFRICAN U20 CHAMPIONSHIPS

The dream
gets a passport.

16.30 metres. African U20 champion. Tenth on the 2017 World Athletics U20 top list. A schoolboy from Harare was now making his mark beyond Zimbabwe.

A bigger runway+

The 2017 African U20 title in Tlemcen, Algeria, marked a new stage. TCU records the gold medal and the 16.30m winning performance. The jump made the next opportunity easier to see: competing and developing in the United States.

04 2018 · FORT WORTH, TEXAS

Belief travels
both ways.

Texas Christian University (TCU), in Fort Worth, offered a full scholarship. Chenge chose the coaches who believed in him before everyone else saw the breakthrough.

Why he chose TCU+

TCU backed him when he was jumping around 15.65m, Chenge recalled in his 2021 interview. He valued that early belief. His first NCAA outdoor season ended with fifth place and 16.42m in 2018. The NCAA is the United States’ college sports system; competing for its Division I titles meant facing a much deeper field.

05 2019 · NCAA OUTDOOR CHAMPIONSHIPS

One last jump.
A national champion.

In Austin, the final attempt changed everything. 17.13 metres. His first NCAA Division I outdoor title.

Composure when it counts+

On 7 June 2019, Chenge won the NCAA outdoor triple jump title with 17.13m on his final attempt. A target he had chased became a result on the board. Later that year, he represented Zimbabwe at the World Championships in Doha.

TCU celebration photograph from the supplied archive; the image’s exact meet and date are unconfirmed.

TCU Athletics · First NCAA title · 7 June 2019 · TCU Magazine · Historical interview · Summer 2021

06 2022 · NCAA OUTDOOR CHAMPIONSHIPS

Back on top.
Further than before.

17.26 metres in Eugene. A second NCAA title, a lifetime best, and a new TCU outdoor record at the time.

The standard behind the result+

The winning mark came on his third attempt on 10 June 2022. TCU’s report, published the following day, records his second national title, fifth Big 12 title and nine All-American honours across his college career. The headline was a distance. Behind it was years of development.

07 AFTER TCU · THREE YEARS OF SELF-DIRECTED TRAINING

My own coach.
A world finalist.

For three years, Chenge coached himself on the ground while working full time. He reached three World Championships—and finished seventh at the 2025 World Indoors.

The chapter that built the academy+

Without the day-to-day structure of college, Chenge took charge of his own development: studying film, planning sessions and carrying the work through alongside a full-time job. Remote feedback and outside expertise still mattered, but there was no coach beside him directing each session. In Nanjing, that persistence reached a world final: 16.74m and seventh place. The experience now shapes the support he builds for other athletes.

The three-year account is Chenge’s recollection. Official records document three later World Championship appearances: Nanjing 2025, Tokyo 2025 and Toruń 2026. His best world-championship placing in that period was seventh indoors at Nanjing.

08 2024–2026 · THE WORLD STAGE

Different stages.
The same Zimbabwe.

African senior silver in 2024. Seventh at the World Indoor Championships in 2025. Still representing Zimbabwe at the World Championships in Tokyo and the 2026 World Indoors.

More than a college chapter+

In Douala, Chenge took African silver with 16.87m. In Nanjing, 16.74m earned seventh in the 2025 World Indoor final. Later that year, he jumped 16.59m in Tokyo’s World Championships qualification. At the 2026 World Indoors in Toruń, he recorded 16.25m. The results changed; the commitment to representing Zimbabwe continued.

HOPSTEPJUMP

09 2026 · DU AIRLINES ELITE ACADEMY

Now the support
travels further.

The lessons became Du Airlines Elite Academy: clearer training, consistent guidance and a pathway for the next generation. Chenge is building it while continuing his own athletic journey.

The journey becomes a system+

The academy grows directly out of that self-coached chapter. Chenge knew what it meant to have knowledge and ambition, yet still need a clear plan for the day. Du Airlines Elite Academy turns those lessons into structured training, movement guidance and coach support for athletes, schools and clubs in Zimbabwe and the United States. The coaching comes first; the platform helps that support travel.

His next ambition is the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics. It is a goal he is working toward, not a qualification already earned.
